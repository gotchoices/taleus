## Observation

Found during the tallysim discovery tier (`tallysim-discovery-harness` / `-trace`), measured on chain graphs `line(3..6)` with ample capacity, zero latency and TallyNet's default hooks. A route of **L** hops is found only when the query's `maxDepth ≥ 2(L − 1)` (for L ≥ 2; a direct peer, L = 1, needs `maxDepth ≥ 1`):

| route hops L | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| smallest `maxDepth` that finds it | 1 | 2 | 4 | 6 | 8 |

So the effective reach at ceiling D is about `⌊D/2⌋ + 1` hops. In every case the target shows up as a **peer match**: the target's neighbour recognises the target among its own tallies and returns the plan.

TallyNet's own `maxDepth` boundary test only checks L = 3 (depth 3 vs. 4), which cannot tell `2(L − 1)` from `maxDepth − 1`, so the behaviour went unnoticed.

## Why it matters

- `maxDepth` is the protocol's main search-cost knob and the parameter the simulator sweeps. If one hop costs two rounds, every search takes about twice the rounds (time budget) it needs, or misses routes the oracle proves exist at that depth.
- tallysim's experiments compare "found" with "oracle at matched depth". Until this is settled, a depth sweep has to budget `maxDepth ≈ 2 × hop distance`, and its found-vs-oracle gap mixes this effect with real protocol losses.

## Task

1. Reproduce with a focused tallynet test (chain graphs, L = 2..6, on the `VirtualClock` through `tallynet/testing`), recording for each L the smallest `maxDepth` that finds the route and the number of originator rounds used.
2. Trace a 4-hop chain round by round: when each node is first visited, what its context `depth` is at each reentrance, where the ceiling check (`participant.ts`, `context.depth >= context.query.maxDepth`, and the originator's `round <= maxDepth` loop) stops the search. Name the mechanism. Likely suspects: a newly visited node spends one reentrance only arming its candidates, so a level costs two rounds; or a node compares its *local* round count with the *global* ceiling, so deeper nodes run out of budget early.
3. Decide, and record the decision in `packages/tallynet/docs/design.md` § Protocol design:
   - **Defect** (expected if the second round per level carries no new information): fix it so a route of L hops is found at `maxDepth = L` (or document an exact off-by-one, e.g. `L − 1` because peer matching resolves the last hop), with one round per level.
   - **Intended** (if there is a real reason one level needs two rounds): document precisely what `maxDepth` counts and give the hop reach as a formula.
4. Make the boundary test exact: for L = 2..6, found at the minimum `maxDepth` and not found at one less.
5. Update tallysim to match: any place that doubles depth to compensate (harness, sweep specs, experiment notes, `packages/tallysim/docs/design.md`) and the tests that assert depth-dependent results.

## Edge cases & interactions

- Direct peer (L = 1), target is self (circular lift), and target found both by peer match and as a visited node.
- Late answers folded into the next round must still be counted against the right level after any change.
- Concurrent sessions on one participant keep separate depth counters.
- Any change to round counting changes time-budget behaviour (`budgetedStep` per round): recheck the sleeping-peer tests and the 1,000-node scale smoke test.
- The originator's loop bound and the participants' ceiling must agree; one source of truth for what a "level" is.
