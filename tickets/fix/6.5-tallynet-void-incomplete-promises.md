## The gap

The simulator's timeout scenarios show it: when a needed participant never promises (dead, unreachable), the lift is **permanently pending** with every promised participant's credit held (`packages/tallysim/docs/design.md` § timeouts, "Abandonment"). The cause:
- A commit or void vote's signed preimage binds the **complete** promise set (`digest.ts`).
- So a voter can't vote at all until every participant has promised.
- With the set never complete, the deadline timer has nothing it can sign.

The only exit today is the schema-side release with a compensation duty (`feat-lift-timeout-release`).

## Why a participant can't just release at the deadline

A participant holding all but one promise at its deadline can't tell two cases apart:
- the set never completed; or
- it completed elsewhere on the route just before the deadline, and a voter majority committed, but the news hasn't arrived yet.

Releasing on that guess could half-apply the lift. The closing word must come from those who could have committed: the voters. MyCHIPs put that word with the originator, which assembled and issued the full record. ChipNet's design had participants sign a "pre-promise void", counted by majority (`../chipnet/doc/cluster.md` § Promise Phase, § Node-level protocol), but never built it (`../chipnet/src/transaction/participant.ts:40` TODO).

## The rule (settled with the developer, 2026-11-27)

**At its deadline, a voter that has not voted commit votes void, whether or not it holds every promise.** A deciding majority of void votes proves the lift can never commit, so every member releases on that proof.

This is safe because an honest voter votes commit only before the deadline and only with the complete promise set, and never votes both ways. Equivocation rules are unchanged.

## Design to settle and build

- **Signature preimage for void.** A commit must keep binding the complete promise set, since it is the proof that everyone promised. A void needs no such proof. Recommended: a void vote's preimage binds the immutable core only, always, so one void face works with or without a complete set. A side benefit is that void votes verify on arrival and need no side pool. The alternative is a separate face type for "void before complete". Pick one and justify it; update `digest.ts`, `signature.ts` and the side-pool logic, and keep "nothing unverified may block a verified vote".
- **Settlement counting.** A void majority settles from the voter set in the topology, whether or not the promise set is complete. A commit majority still requires the complete set.
- **Getting the record to voters.** A voter can only vote void on a lift it knows about. At the deadline, nodes holding an incomplete record must get it to the voters, through contact points and the recovery loop. State what happens when a voter majority never hears of the lift: it stays pending, which is the dead-voters case.
- **Participants.** A participant with an incomplete set never voids on its own. It waits for, and pulls, the voters' decision.
- **Clocks.** Each voter acts at its own deadline plus its skew grace (`SKEW_GRACE_MS`, a per-node setting).

- Update `feat-lift-timeout-release`: a tally releases on verified voter-void evidence with **no** compensation duty. The compensation path remains only for a dead or unreachable voter majority.

## Edge cases & interactions

- A voter that is also the dead participant. A voter set of 1. A threshold above ½.
- A late promise arriving after some voters voided: their void stands; the set completing later changes nothing.
- A voter that committed before the deadline, while others void at the deadline: settle by majority as today.
- A forged void must never count. A void face for a record whose core doesn't match is refused.
