# Scenario: Settings

Source: [42-settings.md](../../../stories/mobile/42-settings.md)

Language, the unit overall figures are read in, and how the app looks — grouped by whether a choice
follows the party or stays on this device.

## Step 1: Which choices follow, and which do not

The story's step 6 asks to be told this, and a party setting up a second phone needs the answer
before they start rather than after. So it is the screen's own structure, not a footnote: language,
display unit and how units are written sit under **Follows you**; appearance sits under **This device
only**.

![Settings](../images/settings-happy.png)

## Step 2: What language does not reach

Two things do not change with the app's language, and both are said where somebody switching
languages will read them. What a counterparty wrote stays in their words, shown as a quote. And a
tally's agreement stays in the language it was signed in — that text *is* the agreement. Where its
publisher offers another language, the version is chosen when the tally is formed, not here.

Only English exists so far. Path A: the party is told what there is, rather than handed a
half-translated app.

## Step 3: The unit overall figures are read in

Each tally keeps counting in what it was agreed in, and the screen says so — that is not this
party's to change. A display unit only decides what the *overall* figures are estimated in, and only
where a rate exists.

Picking Dave-hours is path B: Jan has never priced it, so his overall figures cannot be estimated in
it. He is told so plainly and pointed at rates, rather than being refused the choice.

## Step 4: Mark or code

`$180 50/` or `USD 180 50/` — the same figure, written the way the reader prefers, everywhere in the
app ([`amounts.md`](../../../specs/domain/amounts.md) § Code and mark). Each row shows the figure
rather than describing it, because the choice is about what a figure looks like. MyCHIPs shows
codes; the default here is the mark, because `$180` is what people read fluently.

## Step 5: Appearance, which belongs to this device

Follow the device, always light, or always dark. A replacement phone starts fresh here, and the
screen says that too.

## Step 6: What is not a preference

Path C. What Jan lets a partner owe him is a term of that tally, agreed with them. How value may
move through his tallies is a permission he signs. Neither is a preference, so neither is here — and
a party who comes looking finds that sentence rather than a gap.
