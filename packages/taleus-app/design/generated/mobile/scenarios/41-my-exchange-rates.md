# Scenario: What units are worth to me

Source: [41-my-exchange-rates.md](../../../stories/mobile/41-my-exchange-rates.md)

Saying what a unit is worth — and being told what that actually makes you.

## Step 1: Yours, and only yours

No partner sees a rate, and nobody else's is ever applied to what this party holds. Taleus has no
opinion of its own about what anything is worth, which is why two people can value identical holdings
differently and both be right.

![Rates](../images/exchange-rates.png)

## Step 2: A unit sitting outside everything else, and why

Dave-hours have never been priced, so they are not counted in the overall figures and no payment can
route through them. The tallies themselves work exactly as they always did — a unit you have no
opinion about is one you can still hold and still settle in. That is a coherent place to leave it,
and the screen says so rather than nagging.

## Step 3: Both directions, and which one the figures use

Jan will take a CHIP for $1.46; parting with one costs $1.58. The difference is his own reluctance to
let them go, not a fee anyone charges him. His own figures use the less flattering of the two, and
there is no second set of numbers for looking at his position — the same rates price his trades and
his estimate.

## Step 4: What it applies to

Everything he holds in that unit. It is not a per-partner judgement: valuing something differently
depending on who he is dealing with means a different unit, not a different rate.

## Step 5: Following a source, and what he signed

Rather than a number he must remember to maintain, the CHIP rate follows a published index plus his
own margin. He chose both, and can change both. What he signed, in April, is the *instruction* — not
each day's figure, which is still arriving in September.

## Step 6: What this makes him

Path G, and the reason this screen is not a settings page. Plenty of people hold CHIPs. His rate is
the price value actually converts at through him, and it stands until he changes it. Nobody sees the
number, but anyone trading against him finds it — including people watching that market far more
closely than he is.

So the three ways to limit it sit right there beside it: a wide enough margin, a ceiling on how much
can accumulate this way, or simply not pricing a unit he would rather not trade in.

## Step 7: Valuing and permitting movement are different decisions

Path D. The editor asks them separately. A unit can count toward the overall estimate while nothing
routes across it; permitting movement is a signed permission, and it lives with trading settings.

## Step 8: When the source cannot be reached, and when a number goes stale

The story's own error case, both halves. The index is unreachable, and the last figure it gave is
still what value is converting at. And a flat rate signed in 2024 and never revisited is surfaced as
what it is — a live instruction, not a dormant preference. That is about the party's own neglect, not
about a market Taleus is watching for them.

Dave-hours, priced flat here, is path F: only Jan and Dave use the unit, so a flat number is fine and
needs no source. There is nobody to arbitrage against. The safe case, not a lesser one.

## Not shown

The ceiling on accumulation and the movement permission itself (story 31), deferred until the engine
settles pricing behaviour — the screen names where they live rather than offering a control that does
nothing. A catalogue of sources to choose from: the requirement is that the party chooses the source,
not that the app offers many.
