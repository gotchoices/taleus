# Theory: background for how Taleus works

Not a formal story. This is the plain-language explanation the formal stories assume — what a tally
is and why anyone would want one. It ends where the app begins; the numbered stories take it from
there.

---

Sam: What's that app you're using?

Jan: Taleus!  It's like Venmo, but without the Venmo.

Sam: What do you mean, "without the Venmo"?

Jan: It is just you and the people you transact with, but with no bank in the middle.

Sam: So then who holds the money?

Jan: You, and your partners hold money for each other.

Sam: Like you have to store your friend's cash?  Sounds scary!

Jan: It doesn't work like that.  Think of it this way: Money is just
a way of storing value in the present so we can use it later in the future.

There are two kinds of money:
- Things we use like food, materials, energy.  Commodities
- Value promised to us from others, like an IOU.  Credit

Nowadays most all money is credit money, just value owed from one person to another.  This includes
pretty much all national currencies like the Dollar and the Euro.

We are used to having banks insulate us from the details so we don't have to think about how it
works.  But Taleus allows regular people to interact with each other just like banks deal with each
other--by keeping ledgers of the amounts they owe.

Sam: That sounds amazing, how does it work?

Jan: You can see here.  Each of these is a running account between me and one other person--a
"tally."  Some of them I owe on, and on some of them I'm owed.  Every tally is kept in whatever
we agreed to count in: most of mine are in dollars, this one with my brother is in hours of work.

Sam: So what's your total?

Jan: There isn't really one number.  Each tally stands on its own.  But I can ask the app to
estimate--it says about $1039 if I converted everything at my own rates.  That's my guess, not a
fact.  The real numbers are the ones on each tally.

Sam: I can see that.  But how do you get "real money" into the app?

Jan: Just the same way banks do.  You create it.

Sam: You just create it--out of nothing?

Jan: Not out of nothing, out of something real.  Let's assume you want to buy a bike from me.  I'm
willing to give you the bike now, but I trust you enough to give you a few weeks to pay me the
money.  You give me an IOU for the amount so we can work out actual payment later.

In Taleus, we just record the IOU on our tally and that is literally "the money".  That's how it
gets "into the app."

Sam: Ahh, so the money didn't come from nothing.  It came from the bike!

Jan: Exactly!

Sam: So what happens when you want me to pay you for real?

Jan: In a sense, you already did by giving me the IOU.  With Taleus, I can use that credit for other
things I might want to buy from you.  Let's say you take me out to lunch next week and we want to
split the tab.  We just record the IOU now from me to you and it reduces what you owed me for the
bike.

Sam: Sounds like a bar tab.

Jan: Now you're getting it!  The really cool thing is that when you get connected to other people
besides just me, I can actually "spend" your IOU's not just with you, but with other people you are
connected to, either directly or indirectly.  I can send payments "through you" to other people and
this will also reduce the amount you owe me for the bike.

So it really is spendable--real money--as long as you have enough connections I can send it to for
people I want to buy things from.

And if you don't, I can always call your tab due if I need you to settle the account.  You can send
me Dollars or something else we may find agreeable.

Sam: Wait--what stops me from running up a huge tab and never paying you?

Jan: I do.  When we set up the tally I say how much I'm willing to be owed by you--maybe the price
of the bike and a little more.  That's my limit, and it's my call alone.

Sam: And if I want to sell you something?

Jan: Then you set your own limit on how much you'll let me owe you.  Two separate numbers, each of
us choosing our own.  Mine can be generous and yours can be zero, or the other way around.  That's
really what setting up a tally is: each of us deciding how far we trust the other, in our own
direction, and writing it down.

Sam: That makes sense.  How do we get connected?

Jan: Watch.  (clicks a few prompts on his phone)  Scan this QR code.
